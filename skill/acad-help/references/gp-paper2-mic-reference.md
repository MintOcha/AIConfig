# Canonical 8881 Paper 2 reference

## Official basis

The official syllabus requires candidates to understand how parts of texts relate to convey overall meaning; observe relationships and connections; comprehend, infer, analyse, evaluate, summarise and synthesise; and communicate accurately, coherently and succinctly. It does not define universal command-word formulas.

## Stable method

> Exact idea → required relationship → complete points → MIC

- Treat command words as operations, not fixed abstraction levels.
- Treat teacher answer banks as shorthand until expanded into complete ideas.
- Preserve qualifiers only as parts of complete propositions.
- Accept relevant examples; reject them only when they fail to express the required idea or when the question explicitly demands generalisation.

## Settled specimen interpretations

### Q1: decentralised, self-organised systems

Central idea: behaviour arises without central control through interactions among ants.

Distinct possible points:

- ants interact randomly;
- there is no single decision-maker;
- each ant adjusts its behaviour in response to numerous interactions.

Solving problems beyond an individual ant demonstrates collective intelligence, not decentralisation/self-organisation. Student answer: 1/2 on the supplied teacher marking.

### Q2: groups smarter than individuals

Central idea: human swarming improves decision quality and/or speed by drawing on members' knowledge.

Possible points include optimised decisions/prioritisations/forecasts, significantly less time than traditional methods, and outperforming individual members. The student's “most effective conclusions significantly faster” captures quality and speed and is likely full or near-full credit. Do not penalise it merely because “most” is not a comparative adjective.

### Q3: “Human swarming”

Central idea: people collectively interact like a biological swarm, enabling the group to outperform individual members. Student answer contains both components and is likely 2/2.

### Q4: power of connection

Central idea: connected people and devices exchange information/data in ways that coordinate human and organisational responses for collective benefit.

Possible complete units:

- connected users continuously receive information and broadcast personal data;
- passengers instantly communicate disruptions;
- phones automatically transmit location data;
- real-time updates warn others and alter journeys;
- transport companies detect increased demand and deploy vehicles;
- combined actions/data produce a response that benefits everyone.

These units mix operation, manner, use and result because the question and supplied answer bank are broad. Do not call examples inherently wrong. The student's answer clearly includes warning others, organisational response and collective benefit but omits distinctive continuous/automatic/real-time details. A reasoned estimate is 2/3 absent an exact official scheme; retain any confirmed school mark separately.

### Q5: effective conclusion

Use closing wording → paragraph 1 match → concluding effect.

- “chaos and uncertainty” recalls difficult management problems such as epidemic containment, reinforcing the relevance of swarm-based systems;
- “role model”/“copy” recalls the claim that studying swarm intelligence can help humans create complex systems and turns the argument into a recommendation;
- “humble ant” recalls the opening statement that a single ant is not intelligent, creating a surprising reversal when it becomes a human role model and bringing the argument full circle;
- repeated “we”, if accepted by the school key, inclusively involves writer and reader in the recommendation.

Do not replace “humble” with the unsupported claim that paragraph 1 explicitly calls ants insignificant. The direct contextual basis is the single ant's limited individual intelligence. “Could do worse than” means copying the ant is a reasonably good choice; it does not imply that acting individually prevents humans from reaching their potential.
